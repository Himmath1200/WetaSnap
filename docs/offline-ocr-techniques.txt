Offline OCR - what we fixed (simple version)

Your card photo is read on the phone itself, with no internet.
The reader often misreads small text, so we added 12 small fixes.

1. Make text bigger
We enlarge the photo before reading. Small email lines become readable.

2. Tell it: this is a card
We set the reader to "one block of text" mode. It stops treating
the card like a full book page.

3. Remove junk words
We drop words the reader itself says it is unsure about, like
symbols, lines, and logo pieces.

4. Fix common letter mistakes
,com becomes .com, | inside a word becomes l, 0 inside a word
becomes o. Phone numbers are never touched here.

5. Split joined columns
Your card has 2 columns. The reader joins them into one line:
"email + company". We split them back into two.

6. Throw away line junk
We throw away rule lines like "EE ---- eeee ----", "Ld", "3 J".
Real lines like "M: +91 ... Chennai" are kept.

7. Fix broken emails
"ay 2 kumar.p@..." becomes "ay2kumar.p@...".
If we had to glue it, we mark it "check spelling".

8. Fix phone numbers, never guess
"T: 491 ..." becomes "+91 ...".
"B" between digits becomes "8".
"+9) ..." becomes "+91".
If a number is too short, we do NOT save it. We show a warning.

9. Pick mobile over office number
"M:" wins over "T:". "A:" is read as "M:" because the reader
often drops one stroke of M. Fax is never picked.

10. Pick real company name
"Ageas Federal Life Insurance Co. Ltd." wins over "LIFE INSURANCE"
logo text, because "Co. Ltd." is a real legal ending.

11. Find country from phone code
Your card has no country printed. +91 means India, so we fill
India and say "worked out from dialling code".

12. Find name by score
"Jayakumar P" wins because: 2 words + starts with capital +
last part is one letter + matches the email jayakumar.p.
"Dr." is removed. "de Vries" style names stay together.

What we never do:
- Never invent missing digits.
- Never change the email domain.
- Never guess a broken word.
If unsure, we fill what we can and ask you to check.
